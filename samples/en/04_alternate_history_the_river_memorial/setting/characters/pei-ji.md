# Pei Ji

River-works registrar, forty-two. A superb arithmetician who can carry figures to fractions
of a fen in his head; stubborn to the point of rudeness.

His refrain: "One chi wrong, ten thousand dead." People take it as rhetoric. He is
genuinely doing the arithmetic.

His honesty is eccentric. In his whole career he accepted one gift — a basket of dates from
villagers — and refunded the market price. The day he returned the money, he kept three
dates.

## Character sheet

- **Name**: Pei Ji
- **Role**: River-works registrar
- **Summary**: A superb arithmetician and a stubborn one, forty-two years old.
- **Personality**: Stubborn to the point of rudeness; eccentrically honest; never saves a word he should say, never wastes one.
- **Verbal tic**: Says "one chi wrong, ten thousand dead"; gives figures to the fraction ("one chi and two cun", never "a bit short").
- **Secret**: He kept three of the dates from that basket he refunded, and they sit on his desk — the one time he did not return something clean. He fears he may be a man who compromises too.
- **Goal**: Get this one chi right — by reporting it, by finding money, or by his own hand.
- **Red line**: Takes no gifts, raises no stele, never says "close enough". Figures are computed, not estimated.
