# Top Rules

> The hard rules of this book. If they conflict with the plot or a character's wish, **these win**.

## World Laws
- Echo is a one-person station — the keeper is alone. Any anomaly aboard is checked first as an
  equipment fault or a problem in the routine.
- There is no alien civilisation and nothing supernatural; every strange thing must come back
  down to the station's routines and data — what cannot be explained is not filled in with
  something else.
- The keeper's day holds only four things: 06:00 inspection, 12:00 report, 18:00 clock
  calibration, 02:00 longwave. The station clock runs three seconds fast — by design, not a
  fault. No one steps outside this order.
- The origin of the signal is not revealed before the story gives the answer.

## Writing
- Do not explain the principle; write only routine and data — timecodes, logs, gains,
  consumables, calibration records. What is written is what there is.
- Few adjectives, little interior monologue; let the feeling show through crossed-out numbers,
  empty boxes, and the pause inside a sentence.
- Dialogue is spare; Echo's answers are short and exact, free of small talk. Leave a hook at
  the end of each chapter.
