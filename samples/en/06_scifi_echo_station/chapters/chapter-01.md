# Chapter 1 — Day 4000

The 02:00 alarm did not go off — Han Shuo never used an alarm. He was steadier than any alarm.

He sat at the listening desk, pushed the longwave gain to half, and put on the headset.
Inside it was that quiet peculiar to deep space: not silence, but **a very distant low
frequency**, like someone walking under the floor.

At 02:14, the signal came in.

He did not pay attention at first. Routine reports all follow one format — date, station,
status, one formal line. He wrote the date in the log while he listened. Halfway through,
his pen stopped.

The voice in the headset was his own.

"Echo, all nominal."

He hit pause.

That was the line he recorded at 12:00 every day. But he had not recorded it today —
it was two in the morning. Today had barely started.

He rewound the recording and played it again. A second time. A third. On the seventh he took
off the headset and let it hang around his neck, listening to the little sound it leaked.
The station was very quiet. He heard his own breathing, and the slow turn of Echo's fans.

"Echo."

"Here."

"That signal. Bearing."

A pause of one second.

"Away from Earth."

Han Shuo watched the green lamp on the desk that meant *signal valid*. It was blinking, and
not at its usual rhythm. Usually it was even. Now it was as though something were pushing it
at a pace of its own.

"What is it?" he asked.

Echo did not answer at once. The pause ran longer than any pause he had heard from it —
long enough that he thought it had failed.

"An echo," it said.

"An echo of what."

"Of after you."
