# Chapter 1 — Three to Four

At seven minutes past three, the bell over the door rang.

Zhou Chi did not look up. He was wrapping a 1972 poetry collection — the cover had come
loose and shed a little paper every time it was opened. He could hear it: very faint,
like someone humming a long way off.

The visitor stopped inside the door, closed her umbrella, shook it twice, and leaned it
against the shoe rack. Rainwater ran down the tip and dripped onto the mat.

"There's one making noise today," Zhou Chi said.

"Which one?"

"Back row, second shelf, blue cover — no lettering on the spine. It'll light up as you
walk past."

The woman glanced at him. She did not ask *how do you know*, and she did not smile.
She went over, stopped in front of the second shelf, pulled the book out, leafed through
it, and came back. She put the money on the counter.

One coin over.

Zhou Chi opened his mouth, thought about it, and said nothing. He bagged the book in
brown paper and held it out. She took it, then asked:

"Have you ever had a book you can't hear?"

The bell rang again — the rain had picked up.

"Yes," he said.

"It hasn't gone dim, though. Has it."

Zhou Chi's hand, still half extended, came slowly down. He looked at her for a long
moment. At three in the morning the shop was very quiet, and there was only rain on the
eaves. He heard his own heartbeat. He did not hear the book — but it occurred to him,
suddenly, that this was not a bad thing.

"No," he said. "It hasn't gone dim. Not once."
