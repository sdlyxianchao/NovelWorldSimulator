# Top-level Rules

> This book's hard rules. When they conflict with the plot or a character's wishes,
> **these win.**

## World laws
- The last crossing of the Lantern Festival takes no strangers; the ferryman chooses, and
  whoever he chooses sits in the boat all night. One crossing a year, no more.
- The ferryman and the embroiderer meet exactly once a year. That is a **fact of the
  calendar** — not fate, not a punishment, and never to be written as destiny.
- Whatever is said on the boat, nobody mentions the next day. On that crossing no one sings,
  no one proposes, no one passes anything hand to hand.
- No ghosts, no gods, nothing supernatural. Whatever works on the night of the festival is a
  one-night coincidence, and is never explained as anything else.

## How it is written
- Feeling is never stated outright — it shows only through things and movement: the boat, the
  oar, the lantern, the embroidery frame, the thread, the water.
- Short sentences, plain description, few adjectives; dialogue stays spare, each chapter ends
  on a hook, and there are no summing-up paragraphs and no long passages of inner monologue.
- Not one thread of foreshadowing may be paid off inside a chapter; "once a year" is only
  lived as a matter of days, never explained.
