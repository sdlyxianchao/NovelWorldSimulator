# Chen Du

The ferryman, twenty-five or twenty-six. One river, one boat, and the trade from his
father's time.

He says little and remembers everything — which year it rained on the festival, whose
earring was lost, which year there was one fewer person on the boat.

Along the boat's awning hangs a row of lanterns, one added each year, from bow to stern.
Asked why, he says, "So I can see the river at night." Nobody has ever believed him.

## Character sheet

- **Name**: Chen Du
- **Role**: Ferryman at the landing
- **Summary**: Keeps one river and one boat, rowing as his father did.
- **Personality**: Says little, remembers everything, puts his words into what he does.
- **Verbal tic**: Answers only half a question ("the bridge pier cuts it", "so I can see the river at night"); always gives the count with the year.
- **Secret**: The row of one-lamp-a-year on the awning is for A Su — each one hung on that year's festival. He has never said so, only that it is "so I can see the river at night".
- **Goal**: Run the last crossing every festival, and wait for that one person to board.
- **Red line**: Never presses A Su about her private business; never explains the row of lamps; never takes more than the fare.
