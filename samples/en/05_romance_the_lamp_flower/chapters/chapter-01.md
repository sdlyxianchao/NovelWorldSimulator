# Chapter 1 — The Last Crossing

By the time A Su reached the landing, one line had already been cast off.

"Wait —"

The rower glanced back at her, cast off the second line as well, and set his oar in the
water. The stern dipped and the boat slid back half a foot.

"Get in," he said.

A Su stepped off the bank and dropped in; the boat rocked. She sat down and hugged the cloth
bundle to her chest — a dozen unsold lamp flowers from the day's stall.

The boat left the bank. On shore the lanterns retreated one by one, then became a line, then
a point. The river was quiet; the festival's noise was all upriver. Here there was only
water, and the oar.

"Those lanterns," the rower said suddenly. "Did you put them out?"

A Su followed his gaze. Seven or eight lanterns drifted on the surface, paper ones, some
already sodden, with a little fire still lit under the skin of the water.

"No," she said. "My lanterns don't go in the water."

"Then where do they go?"

"Onto cloth." She opened the bundle a crack and let him see. "In the water they dissolve."

The rower looked for a long moment — long enough that his oar lost half a beat. He said
nothing more, and steered the boat into a bend where a lantern's reflection lay on the
water. The reflection was broken: a gap in the middle, as though a piece had been cut out.

"That one," A Su said, pointing. "Its reflection is broken."

"The bridge pier cuts it," he said.

A Su nodded and hugged the bundle tight again. She wanted to say that she had embroidered
this lantern's reflection, and that it had come out wrong three times. She did not say it.

The boat went on. It was still dark, and there was a long stretch of river before dawn.
