# Top-level Rules

> This book's hard rules. When they conflict with the plot or a character's wishes,
> **these win.**

## World laws
- A clock may be **checked**; it may not be **changed**. Every change is filed, signed
  and sealed — and **an unfiled change is itself a clue**.
- The whole city's hours come out of these few bronze pots: one minute fast in the water
  is one quarter-hour early for the ward. Deviations in time always land on people —
  there are no mere coincidences in this book.
- No ghosts, no gods, no supernatural powers. Every strange thing must end with an
  explanation a reasonable person could actually give.

## How it is written
- **Close one case before opening the next**: each chapter settles its own case; the
  through-line may only be planted, never explained inside a chapter.
- Shen Yan solves cases by *reading the water* — never by a flash of insight, never by
  luck. Every clue must have been shown to the reader first.
- His inner monologue never runs past three sentences in a chapter; his judgement has to
  come out through action and dialogue.
