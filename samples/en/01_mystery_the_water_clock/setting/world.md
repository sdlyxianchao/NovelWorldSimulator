# World

> The Water Clock — Mystery · Case-of-the-week

Tang dynasty, Chang'an. The city does not tell time by the sky; it tells time by the
**night drum**. The night drum is set by the **water clock** of the Astronomical Bureau —
a stack of bronze vessels where water drips from one into the next and a float marks the
scale. Let the water run a little fast, and every ward in the city starts its day early.

The Clock Room is the least regarded office in the Bureau: two scholars, three artisans,
quieter than the watchmen they serve. And yet the opening of markets, the curfew, the
hour of an execution — all of it comes out of those few pots of water.

**The rules of the trade**
- A clock may be **checked**; it may not be **changed**. Any change is filed, signed, sealed.
- Water does not lie. People do. So check the water first, and the people after.
- Anyone who touches the clock must account for where they were — including the Room's own.
