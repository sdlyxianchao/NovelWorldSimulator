# Chapter 1 — A Quarter Hour Early

Shen Yan reached Pingkang Ward just as the third watch drum finished.

He stood in the ward gate and listened. The drum fell; the lamps inside went out one by
one. Too even — not like people blowing out their own lamps, but like someone pressing
the whole ward dark at once.

"A quarter hour early," he said.

The young artisan behind him blinked. "How does the doctor know?"

Shen Yan did not answer. He crouched, took a palm-sized bronze pot from inside his coat,
pulled the stopper, and let water drip into the shallow dish at its base. He watched the
surface and counted under his breath. At one hundred and twenty he stood and stoppered
the pot.

"The clock runs fast," he said. "It isn't the drummer's fault. He strikes by the clock."

The ward was already in disorder. The musician had fallen from a wine-house balcony onto
his own front step; half an hour later the merchant's wife found him drowned in the well.
The inquest men came quickly and wrote quickly: one drunk on his feet, one drawing water
at night.

Shen Yan stood at the well a long time. On the rim there was a faint white line where a
rope had worn the stone — and it sat two finger-widths higher than it should. He reached
into the well. His fingertips touched the water and stopped.

The water was warm.

"This well," he asked the man beside him. "Is it fed by a spring?"

"No. Dead well. Goes cold the moment the weather does."

Shen Yan withdrew his hand and dried it on his sleeve. It was still dark. Chang'an was
still asleep, exactly on the clock's schedule. He looked up toward the Astronomical
Bureau — where the water was moving forward, a little at a time, without pause.
