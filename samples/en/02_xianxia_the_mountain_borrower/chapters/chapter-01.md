# Chapter 1 — The Contract

The contract was spread on a stone table, held down by a paperweight.

The sect's steward ground the ink very thick and brought out a new brush. Clearly he
expected to be writing for a long while. Lu Xingzhou put down two lines and set the
brush aside.

"That is all?" the steward asked.

"That is all." Lu Xingzhou blew on the ink. "What is borrowed, when it comes back, what
condition it comes back in. More than that is useless. More than that is just a place
for someone to wriggle out later."

The steward turned the paper around. The first line read: **Borrow the form of Guanyun
Mountain. Seven days.** The second: **On return, the mountain as it was.**

"Very good," the steward said. "In seven days we will wait for you here."

Lu Xingzhou smiled, folded the contract, tucked it inside his coat, and turned up the
mountain. Halfway up he stopped, drew his hand out of his sleeve and looked at it, palm
up. From the base of the fingers to the wrist ran old marks, one after another, a shade
lighter than the skin around them, as though someone had dragged a fingernail across him
again and again.

He counted from his left hand. When he reached his right wrist, he paused.

Had anyone ever told him that the marks cannot be counted?

Footsteps behind him. A girl in blue cotton stood on the stone step, looking up at him.

"What are you going up for?"

"To measure," he said.

"Measure what?"

"How heavy this mountain is." He walked on. "When you borrow a thing, you had better
first weigh whether you can pay it back."

The girl did not move. She said, "You can't."

Lu Xingzhou's step faltered, then went on. Light came down through the leaves and fell
across his back, and across her face. There was no shadow on her face.
