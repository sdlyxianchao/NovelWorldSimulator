# Lu Xingzhou

Mountain borrower. Looks thirty-odd (nobody can say for certain). His robe is washed
pale, he walks with a swagger, and his smile has no dignity in it whatsoever.

His rules, though, are rigid to the point of pedantry: not one line of the contract
altered, not one day late, and anything borrowed must be washed before it is returned.
His refrain: "Borrowing and not returning is worse than never borrowing."

He has more marks than he can count — because when he counts from his left hand to his
right, he always ends up one over. That last one is **his own lifespan**: to settle his
first debt he put himself up as collateral.

## Character sheet

- **Name**: Lu Xingzhou
- **Role**: Mountain borrower
- **Summary**: A borrowing craftsman in a washed-pale robe; a stricter rule-keeper than anyone.
- **Personality**: Looks idle and grins without dignity; in fact pedantic to the letter — not one line altered, not one day late.
- **Verbal tic**: Opens with the rule ("borrowing and not returning is worse than never borrowing"); always gives the term with the day count ("seven days, not one more").
- **Secret**: The extra mark on his hands is his own lifespan — to settle his first debt he put himself up as collateral.
- **Goal**: Settle this mountain account cleanly, and work out whether A Ruo counts as part of the mountain.
- **Red line**: Not one line of the contract altered, not one day late; anything borrowed is washed before it goes back. Never pays his own debt with someone else's years.
