# Top-level Rules

> This book's hard rules. When they conflict with the plot or a character's wishes,
> **these win.**

## World laws
- In the cultivation world nothing is *taken* — only *borrowed*. What is borrowed must be
  returned; what cannot be returned goes on the ledger, written on the borrower.
- Borrowing a mountain means borrowing its *form*. Take the form and the mountain does not
  fall, but the people inside lose where they came from — and somehow the loan always lands
  on a person.
- Not one line of the contract is altered, not one day is late; every extra line is only a
  place to wriggle out of paying.
- The keeper of the mountain is not counted in the loan. When the mountain is borrowed, the
  keeper is not counted as part of what is taken.

## How it is written
- Short sentences, plain description; write only *measuring* and *returning*, never feeling.
  Lu Xingzhou always speaks in numbers and days, never about himself.
- Every "power" stays inside the ledger of borrowing — no conjuring things from nothing, no
  boundless magic.
- End each chapter on a hook; plant foreshadowing but never pay it off inside a chapter.
  A Ruo's missing shadow may only be shown, never explained.
